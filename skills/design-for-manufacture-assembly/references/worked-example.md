# Worked example: fewer fasteners, with a sealing question

All times, prices, quantities, and design details are synthetic. The revised design has not been built or tested.

## Baseline and proposal

A cooler lid assembly uses 12 small fasteners. Five observed baseline assemblies took 176, 182, 180, 179, and 183 seconds, an average of 180 seconds. A proposed arrangement uses four captive fasteners. The team estimates 140 seconds per assembly and a recurring parts saving of $0.12 per unit, but has not measured the revised operation. A new fixture is estimated at $260. The illustrative labor rate is $36 per hour.

## Comparative model

Predicted labor saving per unit:

```text
(180 - 140) s × ($36 / 3600 s) = $0.40
```

Predicted recurring saving is $0.40 + $0.12 = **$0.52 per unit**. With only the stated incremental fixture cost included:

```text
Net saving at quantity N = $0.52 N - $260
Break-even quantity = $260 / $0.52 = 500 units
```

At 100 units, the proposal costs $208 more under this model. At 1,000 units, it saves $260. These are conditional cost comparisons, not a production forecast or supplier quote. The simple model excludes other development, qualification, maintenance, scrap, and rework changes.

## Engineering tradeoff

Reducing the number of fasteners changes the load distribution on the lid and seal. The proposal might simplify handling but worsen sealing, local deflection, or service access. The fastener count therefore does not establish the preferred design.

Before selection, review the lid geometry and sealing requirements; evaluate load and seal compression; then observe assembly of the revised design and test relevant sealing and service conditions. Retain the original timing records and identify the design/process revision used for every comparison.

## Handoff

Phase 3 can define the proposed geometry and process change. Phase 4 should validate its functional and assembly consequences. If adopted for Phase 5, update the fixture, operator instructions, and quality checks, and measure performance on the actual process. Until that evidence exists, retain the 140-second cycle as an estimate.
