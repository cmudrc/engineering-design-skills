# Product Planning

Define a worthwhile, bounded development effort while leaving useful solution choices open. Planning may compare several project opportunities or clarify the mission of an already selected one.

## Establish the opportunity

Start with the user's brief. Identify whose situation should improve, what benefit is sought, why the organization should pursue it, and what evidence supports that opportunity. Separate the buyer, user, maintainer, and production stakeholders where their interests differ. Ask the question that most affects scope; do not require a complete business case before making progress.

Distinguish product planning from scheduling an already defined engineering project. If the project has been selected, preserve that decision and concentrate on its mission rather than reopening unrelated portfolio choices.

## Develop the plan

1. **Clarify the opportunity and context.** Describe the target situation and desired benefit without prematurely making an architecture, material, or mechanism mandatory. Record genuine platform, manufacturing, service, or environmental constraints and their sources.
2. **Assess priorities and fit.** When several opportunities are in scope, compare them against explicit organizational goals, stakeholder value, strategic fit, technical uncertainty, and available resources. Keep missing evidence visible. A single ungrounded weighted score is not a substitute for a prioritization rationale.
3. **Check resource and timing assumptions.** Identify the skills, facilities, budget information, dependencies, and decision dates actually supplied. State what the proposed concept-development effort can reasonably resolve. For a novel product, plan the next learning stage at an appropriate resolution rather than inventing a detailed production schedule.
4. **Draft the mission.** Capture the product description, benefit proposition, business or organizational goals, primary and secondary markets, stakeholders, and assumptions or constraints. Keep measurable aspirations separate from confirmed requirements. State what remains intentionally open for Phase 1.
5. **Review consistency and the next decision.** Check whether scope, benefit, resources, and constraints fit together. Identify assumptions that could invalidate the project. Recommend the next investigation and the evidence that would support proceeding, changing scope, or deferring work.

Use [assets/mission-statement.md](assets/mission-statement.md) for the output and [references/worked-example.md](references/worked-example.md) for an illustrative mission with unresolved evidence.

## Quality checks and handoff

- Distinguish observed demand from a hypothesis about demand. Do not invent completed interviews, market size, budgets, or management approval.
- Identify conflicts between stakeholder needs, business aims, technical constraints, and available resources.
- Challenge an assumed solution only when doing so opens a meaningful opportunity; preserve explicit user commitments and platform requirements.
- Record recommendations separately from decisions made by the project owner.
- Hand Phase 1 a usable mission and a small set of learning questions, not a claim that the product concept is already selected.

Phase context: **0, Planning**. Revisit the mission if later evidence materially changes the opportunity.
