# Worked example: a pilot lot that needed rework

All production data and circumstances below are synthetic. This is a readiness review, not a certification or a statistical capability study.

## Brief and process basis

The team has built 30 coolers using the intended parts, fixtures, assembly route, and trained operators. It wants to double the next lot. Logged elapsed time and staffing are missing. The product revision and inspection method are identified, but long-term process stability has not been established.

## Reconcile the lot

| Unit disposition | Count |
|---|---:|
| Entered the defined pilot process | 30 |
| Conforming at the first inspection, without rework | 24 |
| Initially failed sealing; reworked and subsequently conforming | 4 |
| Failed bracket geometry; scrapped | 2 |
| Final held or unknown | 0 |

First-pass accounting is 24 conforming + 4 rework units + 2 scrap units = 30 entered units. Final accounting is 28 conforming + 2 scrapped = 30. The four reworked units are part of the 28 finally conforming units; do not count them again.

```text
First-pass yield = 24 / 30 = 80.0%
Final-conformance ratio = (24 + 4) / 30 = 93.3%
Scrap fraction = 2 / 30 = 6.7%
```

The final ratio does not mean that 93.3% passed without intervention. Rework consumed effort and may conceal a problem that will become a bottleneck at higher volume.

## Readiness assessment

The lot supports a review of the actual process, but its counts alone do not establish an achievable production rate, stable capability, or future yield. Do not calculate a capability index from the 80% figure. There is no stated basis for a universal “good enough” threshold.

Prioritize investigating the seal failures and bracket geometry. Trace affected units to the relevant station, part/fixture revision, material, and operator records. The defect categories suggest where to investigate; they do not prove an operator or fixture caused the problem.

## Proposed next step

Before doubling the lot, verify targeted changes in a bounded run using the intended process. Preserve unit disposition and revision records, check operator work instructions, and collect elapsed time, staffing, rework effort, and relevant quality measurements. Compare that evidence with the project's actual readiness and output criteria. Keep the recommendation separate from the owner's decision to proceed.
