# Worked example: one cooler warms early

All units, observations, and criteria below are synthetic teaching data. The follow-up experiment is proposed, not completed.

## Decision and evidence

The team wants to freeze a developed cooler design. Three independently built units use the intended geometry and materials, assembled in the workshop. They are tested with a 5 L payload, closed lid, 4 degrees C initial payload, 30 degrees C ambient, and prescribed coolant. The stated criterion is payload temperature no greater than 10 degrees C after six hours.

| Unit | Six-hour payload temperature | Result |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | 9.4 degrees C | Meets criterion in this run |
| P2 | 9.8 degrees C | Meets criterion in this run |
| P3 | 10.6 degrees C | Does not meet criterion in this run |

Two of three units meet the criterion in the stated tests. Three observations do not establish a population reliability or production capability. They also do not explain P3's result.

## Learning question and fidelity

Before freezing the design, determine whether the difference is associated with a measurement/setup issue, a particular lid/seal assembly, or the remaining container assembly. The prototypes must preserve the relevant thermal interfaces and test conditions; cosmetic details are not necessary for this question.

## Proposed follow-up

First check sensor agreement against a common reference and review payload, coolant preparation, placement, timing, and logged ambient conditions. Do not dismiss P3's observation merely because a setup error is possible.

If these checks support valid comparisons, use P1 and P3 in a paired lid-interchange study. Test each body with each compatible lid assembly under the same documented protocol, retaining unit/lid identities, design revision, sensor locations, preparation, and run order. Balance or randomize run order where practical. Confirm that swapping the lids does not itself alter other relevant interfaces.

Repeated runs help assess run-to-run variation; the two bodies and two lids remain a very small sample. This study can help localize a problem. It is not a reliability demonstration or a guarantee that the inferred mechanism generalizes.

## Interpretation and handoff

If results track a lid assembly, inspect its seal and geometry and test a specific corrective hypothesis. If they track a body or remain inconsistent, revise the hypotheses. Preserve all results, including failures and invalidated runs with their reasons.

Keep the 10-degree criterion fixed while interpreting this study. After a proposed change, repeat the relevant tests across affected units and conditions rather than keeping only the two original passing samples. Phase 4 design refinement can follow; production-system readiness remains a separate Phase 5 question.
